Definition. Generalized eigenvector.
Try to state it, then click to flip
Definition. Let
  • $V$ be a finite-dimensional nonzero vector space over $\mathbb F$.
  • $T\in\mathcal L(V)$.
  • $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $T$.
A vector $v\in V$ is a generalized eigenvector of $T$ corresponding to $\lambda$ if $v\neq 0$ and there exists a positive integer $k$ such that$$ (T-\lambda I)^k v=0. $$
NoteEquivalently, for an eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $T$, a nonzero $v\in V$ is a generalized eigenvector of $T$ corresponding to $\lambda$ if and only if $(T-\lambda I)^{\dim V}v=0$.
Theorem. Cayley-Hamilton theorem (complex case).
Try to state it, then click to flip
Theorem. Let
  • $\mathbb F=\mathbb C$.
  • $V$ be a finite-dimensional nonzero vector space over $\mathbb F$.
  • $T\in\mathcal L(V)$.
  • $q$ be the characteristic polynomial of $T$.
Then$$ q(T)=0. $$
Theorem. Jordan form.
Try to state it, then click to flip
Theorem. Let
  • $V$ be a finite-dimensional nonzero complex vector space.
  • $T\in\mathcal L(V)$.
  • $n=\dim V$.
Then $T$ has a Jordan basis $v_1,\ldots,v_n$.
NoteHere $J_r(\lambda)$ denotes the $r\times r$ matrix with diagonal entries $\lambda$ and superdiagonal entries $1$, all other entries equal to $0$.
