Definition. Let
  • $V$ be a vector space over $\mathbb F$.
  • $T\in\mathcal L(V)$.
A scalar $\lambda\in\mathbb F$ is an eigenvalue of $T$ if$$ \exists v\in V:\ v\neq 0\ \text{and}\ Tv=\lambda v. $$
NoteThe condition $v\neq 0$ is essential because $T0=\lambda 0$ for every $\lambda\in\mathbb F$.

  • $V$ be a finite-dimensional nonzero complex vector space.
  • $T\in\mathcal L(V)$.
Then $T$ has an eigenvalue.
Theorem. Eigenvalues are the zeros of the minimal polynomial.
Try to state it, then click to flip
Theorem. Let
  • $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb F$.
  • $T\in\mathcal L(V)$.
  • $p$ be the minimal polynomial of $T$.
Then, for every $\lambda\in\mathbb F$,$$ p(\lambda)=0\Longleftrightarrow \lambda\text{ is an eigenvalue of }T. $$
