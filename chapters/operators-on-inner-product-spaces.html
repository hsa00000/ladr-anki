Definition. Self-adjoint.

Definition. Let
  • $V$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional inner product space over $\mathbb F$.
  • $T\in\mathcal L(V)$.
Then $T$ is self-adjoint if$$ T=T^*. $$
NoteEquivalently, $\langle Tv,w\rangle=\langle v,Tw\rangle$ for all $v,w\in V$.
Theorem. Complex spectral theorem.
Try to state it, then click to flip
Theorem. Let
  • $V$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner product space.
  • $T\in\mathcal L(V)$.
Then the following are equivalent.
1. $T$ is normal.
2. $\exists$ orthonormal basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $V$ such that $\mathcal M(T,(e_1,\ldots,e_n))$ is diagonal.
3. There is an orthonormal basis of $V$ consisting of eigenvectors of $T$.
Theorem. Singular value decomposition.
Try to state it, then click to flip
Theorem. Let
  • $V$ and $W$ be nonzero finite-dimensional inner product spaces over $\mathbb F$.
  • $T\in\mathcal L(V,W)$.
  • $m=\operatorname{rank}T$.
  • $s_1,\ldots,s_m$ be the positive singular values of $T$, in nonincreasing order (empty if $m=0$).
Then there exist orthonormal lists $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ in $V$ and $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ in $W$ (empty if $m=0$) such that$$ Tv=s_1\langle v,e_1\rangle f_1+\cdots+s_m\langle v,e_m\rangle f_m $$ for every $v\in V$.
NoteThe lists can be extended to orthonormal bases of $V$ and $W$, with respect to which $T$ has a diagonal matrix (in the rectangular sense).
