Definition. Inner product.
Try to state it, then click to flip
Definition. Let
  • $V$ be a vector space over $\mathbb F$, where $\mathbb F=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb F=\mathbb C$.
  • $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb F$ be a function.
Then $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is an inner product on $V$ if all of the following hold:
1. Positivity: $\langle v,v\rangle\ge 0$ for all $v\in V$.
2. Definiteness: $\langle v,v\rangle=0$ if and only if $v=0$.
3. Additivity in the first slot: $\langle u+v,w\rangle=\langle u,w\rangle+\langle v,w\rangle$ for all $u,v,w\in V$.
4. Homogeneity in the first slot: $\langle\lambda u,v\rangle=\lambda\langle u,v\rangle$ for all $\lambda\in\mathbb F$ and all $u,v\in V$.
5. Conjugate symmetry: $\langle u,v\rangle=\overline{\langle v,u\rangle}$ for all $u,v\in V$.
NoteWhen $\mathbb F=\mathbb C$, the notation $\lambda\ge 0$ means that $\lambda$ is real and nonnegative. If $\mathbb F=\mathbb R$, conjugate symmetry reduces to $\langle u,v\rangle=\langle v,u\rangle$ for all $u,v\in V$.
Theorem. Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Theorem. Let
  • $V$ be an inner product space over $\mathbb F$.
  • $u,v\in V$.
Then$$ |\langle u,v\rangle|\le\|u\|\|v\|. $$ Equality holds if and only if the list $u,v$ is linearly dependent.
Theorem-Definition. Gram-Schmidt procedure.
Try to state it, then click to flip
Theorem-Definition. Let
  • $V$ be an inner product space over $\mathbb F$.
  • $v_1,\ldots,v_m$ be a linearly independent list of vectors in $V$.
The Gram-Schmidt procedure produces a list $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ as follows.
  • If $m=0$, the output is the empty list.
  • If $m\ge1$, set $f_1=v_1$ and, for $k=2,\ldots,m$,
    • $$ f_k=v_k-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{\langle v_k,f_j\rangle}{\|f_j\|^2}f_j,\qquad e_k=\frac{f_k}{\|f_k\|}, $$
and set $e_1=f_1/\|f_1\|$.
Then $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ is an orthonormal list and $\operatorname{span}(v_1,\ldots,v_k)=\operatorname{span}(e_1,\ldots,e_k)$ for $k=1,\ldots,m$.
