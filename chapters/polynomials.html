Theorem. Let
  • $\mathbb F$ be $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$.
  • $p,s\in\mathcal P(\mathbb F)$.
  • $s\neq0$.
Then there exist unique $q,r\in\mathcal P(\mathbb F)$ such that$$ p=sq+r $$ and $\operatorname{deg}r<\operatorname{deg}s$.
NoteIf $p=0$, then $q=r=0$; the condition $\operatorname{deg}r<\operatorname{deg}s$ holds because $\operatorname{deg}0=-\infty$.

Theorem. Let
  • $p\in\mathcal P(\mathbb C)$ be a nonconstant polynomial.
  • $m=\operatorname{deg}p$.
Then $p$ has a unique factorization (except for the order of the factors)$$ p(z)=c(z-\lambda_1)\cdots(z-\lambda_m), $$ where $c\in\mathbb C$ is the coefficient of $z^m$ in $p$ and $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\in\mathbb C$.
NoteThe numbers $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m$ are precisely the zeros of $p$.
